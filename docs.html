How it works

Parcel is a launchpad on Robinhood Chain. Every launch is tethered to a property class for flavor — a shed, a couch, a villa, a high-rise unit — but trading itself is always plain ETH. Connect a wallet, send ETH, get tokens. Nothing to mint or approve first, for the creator or for anyone buying after them.

Property classes

Each of the 20 classes on the class list is a plain string tag — "SHED", "VILA", "COUCH" — attached to a launch when it's created. It shows up in the UI and in the launch's on-chain metadata, and that's the whole extent of what it does. There's no per-class coin, no oracle a trade depends on, and no step where a buyer needs to hold anything but ETH before they can buy in. A joke class and a serious one go through the exact same contract calls.

PriceOracle.sol still exists in the repo as an optional, currently-unused piece — a place to eventually hang a real "this launch is worth roughly N sheds" style display, fed by comparable sales or listing data. Nothing in the live contracts reads from it today.

Launch terms

Every launch follows the same curve, whatever class it's tagged with.

Mint and open

A launch mints 1,000,000,000 tokens. 800 million sell on a virtual-liquidity curve priced directly in ETH, using fixed virtual reserves (3 ETH / 1,073,000,000 tokens) set once in the contract — not derived from any price feed. That opens the curve around a 2.8 ETH implied cap. 200 million tokens are reserved for migration.

Creator sets the fee and buys first

The creator picks a trading fee between 1% and 3% and sends their first buy — any amount of ETH — in the same transaction that creates the market, so a market never exists with zero buyers. No approval step, because there's nothing to approve: the ETH goes straight into the constructor call.

Trade on the curve

Anyone can buy or sell against the curve while it's open, straight in ETH. Price moves along a constant-product virtual curve. If a buy would sell more tokens than the curve has left, it's automatically capped at the remaining supply and the unused ETH is refunded in the same transaction — it doesn't revert on you for sending slightly too much.

Migrate

When the curve sells out, the ETH raised and the reserved 200 million tokens move into a Uniswap v4 pool in the same transaction, at the curve's final price. The pool charges the same fee the creator chose, enforced by the pool itself, and its liquidity is permanent — it isn't withdrawable by the creator or by Parcel.

Fees

Every fee is split the same way on the curve and in the pool after migration, always in ETH:

RecipientSharePaid inHow
Creator30%ETHClaimed from the account page, any time.

Market metadata

A launch stores its image, name, and links in content-addressed storage. The permanent metadata URI is written on chain at creation and never changes, so a market's name, image, and links stay exactly what its creator published — including for the joke listings.

What this repo doesn't do yet

The contracts can be deployed for real to Robinhood Chain Testnet (see DEPLOY.md) and this site will pick that up automatically. Even deployed, some things are still simplified:

  • The contracts are unaudited reference code — testnet only. Nothing here should hold mainnet funds as-is.
  • Holder fees are claim-based, not pushed to every holder automatically — see the fees table above. Pushing a transfer to every holder on every trade doesn't scale gas-wise; a production front end should surface a "claim" button rather than imply it happens with no action.
  • There's no real Uniswap v4 pool behind migration on testnet — TestnetMigrator just holds the raised ETH and reserved tokens so a sellout doesn't revert. A migrated market has nowhere to trade after sellout until a real AMM integration replaces it.
  • Market images and descriptions aren't pinned to IPFS — the launch form encodes them into an inline data URI, which is fine for a demo and not for anything meant to last.
  • There's no markets index — index.html shows the static class list, not live launches. Finding a specific market means reading LaunchCreated events or checking the explorer directly.
  • Nothing here evaluates whether tokenizing a specific physical property makes the resulting coin a security, a real-estate-adjacent financial product, or something requiring registration in your jurisdiction. That's a legal question, not a code path — talk to a lawyer before this touches real money or real property titles.
