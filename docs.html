CASTLE is a launchpad on Robinhood Chain Testnet. Buying and selling on a curve always happens in plain ETH, connect a wallet, send ETH, get tokens, nothing to approve or mint first. Optionally tethering a launch to a property class doesn't change that; it only changes what migration seeds once the curve sells out.

Property-class coins

Each of the 20 classes on the class list, plus USDG, is a real, deployed PropertyClassCoin, not just a label. Every one works the same way: mint() takes ETH and issues coin at a fixed, immutable rate set once at deployment; redeem() burns coin and gives back exactly the ETH that backs it. There's no oracle and nothing updates the rate. It's fully collateralized: minting only ever issues coin backed by ETH just deposited, so the peg can't run short. You can buy and sell any of these directly on the classes page, independent of launching anything.

The starting USD reference price for each class was researched, not guessed, averaged across 2 to 4 independent sources for the 10 classes with a real market (RV, tiny homes, manufactured homes, container homes, cabins, condos, houses, farmland, luxury homes, commercial retail). Duplex, townhouse, and high-rise prices are derived from the house/condo figures with a disclosed multiplier rather than independently sourced, and farmland applies a disclosed 3x multiplier for structures since no source prices "land with structures" directly. The six novelty-tier classes (couch, tent, shed, lean-to, van, shanty) have no real market to research and stay illustrative. Full source list, per class, is in script/Deploy.s.sol's comments.

Launch terms

Every launch follows the same curve, whatever class it's tethered to.

Mint and open

A launch mints 1,000,000,000 tokens. 800 million sell on a virtual-liquidity curve priced directly in ETH, using fixed virtual reserves (3 ETH / 1,073,000,000 tokens) set once in the contract, not derived from any price feed, and identical whether or not a property class is picked. That opens the curve around a 2.8 ETH implied cap. 200 million tokens are reserved for migration.

Creator sets the fee, picks a class (or not), and buys first

The creator picks a trading fee between 1% and 3%, optionally picks a property class to tether the launch to, and sends their first buy, any amount of ETH, in the same transaction that creates the market. No approval step either way: the ETH goes straight into the constructor call.

Trade on the curve

Anyone can buy or sell against the curve while it's open, always in ETH, a picked class never changes this part. If a buy would sell more tokens than the curve has left, it's automatically capped at the remaining supply and the unused ETH is refunded in the same transaction, rather than reverting.

Migrate into one pool, or two

When the curve sells out: with no class picked, the full 200 million reserved tokens and all raised ETH move into a single TOKEN/ETH pool. With a class picked, it splits down the middle: half stays plain ETH for a TOKEN/ETH pool, and the other half gets minted into the class coin first (at its fixed rate, using real ETH) before seeding a genuinely class-coin-backed TOKEN/<class> pool. Both pools carry the fee the creator chose, and neither is ever withdrawable.

Fees

Every fee is split the same way on the curve and in the pool(s) after migration, always in ETH. There's no holder-reward bucket, nothing accrues to a wallet just for holding a token, and there's nothing to claim on that basis:

Market metadata

A launch stores its image, name, and links in content-addressed storage. The permanent metadata URI is written on chain at creation and never changes, so a market's name, image, and links stay exactly what its creator published, including for the joke listings.

What this repo doesn't do yet

The contracts can be deployed for real to Robinhood Chain Testnet, and this site picks that up automatically. Even deployed, some things are still simplified:

  • Every property-class coin's rate is fixed once at deploy time and doesn't update on its own. A real deployment would want a live price feed behind each class, and real USDG should just be the actual USDG stablecoin.
  • There's no real Uniswap v4 pool behind migration on testnet. TestnetMigrator just holds whatever it's given (ETH, and a class coin if one was picked) so a sellout doesn't revert. A migrated market has nowhere to trade after sellout until a real AMM integration replaces it. This applies to both pools when a class was picked, not just one.
  • The price chart plots implied price per trade in trade sequence, not real timestamps (avoids an RPC call per trade), and it's a simple line, no candles, no OHLC aggregation, no zoom/pan.
  • Launch images are real now, resized client-side and embedded directly in the on-chain metadata, but they still aren't pinned to IPFS or any external host, and they're capped small (a few KB) specifically because a launch's metadata is a string in contract calldata, so gas cost scales with its size.
  • Nothing here evaluates whether tokenizing a specific physical property makes the resulting coin a security, a real-estate-adjacent financial product, or something requiring registration in your jurisdiction.
